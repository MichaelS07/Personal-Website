## Line 1 — Hook (Frame 1)

**Time:** 0:00 – 0:28
**Delivery:** Direct, a little knowing.

    Your cost per click was two dollars ten last month. This month it's two ninety-five. That's a forty percent jump, and your budget hasn't changed, so you're getting fewer clicks for the same money. The usual reaction is to panic, drop bids, or blame Google. Don't. A rising CPC isn't the problem. It's a symptom. And in the next ten minutes, I'll show you how to find the actual cause, and fix it.

## Line 2 — The promise (Frame 2)

**Time:** 0:28 – 0:50
**Delivery:** Lay out the road map.

    Here's the plan. First, we check whether it's even a problem. Then we look at how Google actually sets your CPC, because once you see the formula, every cause makes sense. Then we go through the six things that push CPC up, where to find each one in your account, and how to fix it. And at the end, a fifteen minute checklist you can run every time this happens.

## Line 3 — Is it actually a problem? (Frame 3)

**Time:** 0:50 – 1:50
**Delivery:** Reassuring, then firm.

    Step one. Before you touch anything, look past CPC. CPC is a cost metric. It doesn't tell you if you're making money. Put cost per click next to conversion rate and cost per acquisition. Say your CPC went from two ten to two ninety-five. But your conversion rate also went from four percent to six percent. Your cost per acquisition actually dropped, from fifty-two fifty to about forty-nine. You're paying more per click, but those clicks are better, and you're getting customers cheaper. That's not a problem. That's Smart Bidding doing its job, paying more for people who are more likely to buy. So the real question is: did your C P A or your ROAS get worse? If not, leave it alone. If it did, keep watching, because now we need to find out why.

## Line 4 — How CPC is actually set (Frame 4)

**Time:** 1:50 – 3:05
**Delivery:** Teacher mode. Slow down on the formula.

    Every time someone searches, Google runs an auction. But it's not won by the highest bid. It's won by the highest Ad Rank. Ad Rank is roughly your bid, multiplied by your Quality Score, plus things like ad assets and the context of the search. And here's the part most people miss. You don't pay your bid. In the classic version of the formula, you pay just enough to beat the advertiser below you. Your actual CPC equals the Ad Rank of the advertiser below you, divided by your Quality Score, plus one cent. Look at what that means. There are really only three ways your CPC goes up. The people below you get stronger, so the number on top rises. Your Quality Score drops, so the number on the bottom shrinks. Or your own bidding pushes you into pricier auctions. Every cause in this video is one of those levers. Keep this formula in your head.

## Line 5 — Cause 1: More competition (Frame 5)

**Time:** 3:05 – 4:05
**Delivery:** Investigative.

    Cause number one. More competition. A new competitor enters your auctions, or an existing one raises their budget. The advertiser below you gets stronger, so you pay more. Here's where to check. Go to Insights and reports, then Auction insights. Set the date range to compare this period with the last one. Look for a new name in the list, or a competitor whose overlap rate or outranking share jumped. If someone went from twenty percent overlap to sixty, that's your answer. The fix is not to get into a bidding war. You'll both lose, and Google wins. Instead, raise your Quality Score, which we'll cover next, so you beat them without paying more. Find where you still win, by device, location or time of day, and push budget there. And if they're bidding on your brand name, protect your brand campaign. It's usually the cheapest traffic you'll ever buy.

## Line 6 — Cause 2: Quality Score dropped (Frame 6)

**Time:** 4:05 – 5:10
**Delivery:** Precise.

    Cause number two. Your Quality Score dropped. Remember, it's the number on the bottom of the formula. When it falls, your CPC rises, even if nothing else changes. To check it, go to your Keywords view, and add four columns: Quality Score, Expected click through rate, Ad relevance, and Landing page experience. Then add the historical versions of those columns, so you can see when it changed. Each part gets a rating of below average, average, or above average. That tells you exactly what to fix. Below average expected CTR? Your ads aren't compelling. Test new headlines, and add sitelinks and callouts. Below average ad relevance? Your ad groups are too broad. Split them up so the ad matches the search. Below average landing page? The page is slow, or doesn't match what the ad promised. Check it on mobile. A page that takes five seconds to load is costing you money on every click.

## Line 7 — Cause 3: Your bidding changed (Frame 7)

**Time:** 5:10 – 6:10
**Delivery:** Slightly wry: sometimes the call is coming from inside the house.

    Cause number three. Your own bidding. Sometimes the CPC increase is self inflicted. Go to Tools, then Change history, and filter by bid and budget changes. Look for three things. One, a budget increase on Maximise conversions. With no target set, Google will spend that extra money by bidding into more expensive auctions. Two, a raised target C P A, or a lowered target ROAS. You've told Google it's allowed to pay more, so it does. Three, a switch to target impression share, especially absolute top of page. That strategy chases position, not profit, and it'll pay whatever it takes. Also check the bid strategy status. If it says learning, CPCs often jump around for about a week. The fix: set realistic targets, change them in small steps of around fifteen to twenty percent, and give each change time before you judge it.

## Line 8 — Cause 4: Broad match drift (Frame 8)

**Time:** 6:10 – 7:05
**Delivery:** Show, don't tell. Let the search terms do the talking.

    Cause number four. Your keywords are matching expensive searches. Broad match, and Performance Max, decide for themselves which searches you show up for. Over time, they can drift into queries that are more competitive, and often less relevant. You bid on "running shoes," and suddenly you're paying for "Nike Pegasus 41 sale," competing with every big retailer in the country. To check, open Insights and reports, then Search terms. Sort by cost, and look at the top fifty. For each one, ask: would I have chosen this keyword myself? The fix is to add negative keywords for anything irrelevant, move your best converting searches into their own exact or phrase match keywords, and make this a weekly habit, not a once a year clean up.

## Line 9 — Cause 5: Seasonality (Frame 9)

**Time:** 7:05 – 7:50
**Delivery:** Matter of fact.

    Cause number five. Seasonality. Some months are just more expensive. In the run up to Black Friday and Christmas, more businesses bid on the same searches, and CPCs can climb thirty percent or more across a whole industry. To check, compare the same dates against last year, not just last month. If last November had the same jump, it's seasonal. The fix is not to fight the market. Plan for it. Adjust your targets ahead of time, move budget towards your highest margin products, and judge success on return on ad spend, not cost per click.

## Line 10 — Cause 6: Broken conversion tracking (Frame 10)

**Time:** 7:50 – 8:45
**Delivery:** Serious. This is the sneaky one.

    Cause number six. And this one catches a lot of people. Your conversion tracking is broken. Smart Bidding learns from your conversion data. If that data is wrong, it bids wrong. The most common example is a conversion that fires twice. Google thinks each click is worth double, so it bids higher and your CPC climbs. The same thing happens when a low value action, like a page view, is set as a primary conversion. To check, go to Goals, then Conversions summary. Look at every primary action. Is the status recording properly? Do the numbers match what your shop or CRM says actually happened? If Google says forty sales and Shopify says twenty, you've found your problem. Fix the tag, remove duplicates, and set only real business outcomes as primary.

## Line 11 — The 15-minute checklist (Frame 11)

**Time:** 8:45 – 9:40
**Delivery:** Brisk, practical. A list to screenshot.

    So, next time your CPC jumps, run this checklist. Minute one: check C P A and ROAS. If they're fine, stop there. Minutes two to four: change history. Did anyone change budgets, targets, or bid strategy? Minutes four to six: segment by campaign, and find where the increase is actually happening. Minutes six to eight: auction insights, for new competitors. Minutes eight to ten: Quality Score and its history. Minutes ten to thirteen: search terms, sorted by cost. And minutes thirteen to fifteen: conversion tracking, compared against your real sales. Last of all, compare the same period last year to rule out seasonality.
