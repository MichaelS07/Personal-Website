Stop Discounting Abandon Carts Before Q4 — Fix Freight and GST First

September 14, 2026 • Lifecycle•7 min read

If you run a Shopify store in New Zealand or Australia, September is when you should fix abandon-cart recovery — not November, when paid is expensive and every competitor is blasting the same 15% code.

Most NZ/AU stores do not have a “Klaviyo problem.” They have a truth problem. The customer sees a clean product price on the PDP, hits checkout, then meets freight, GST, or a rural surcharge for the first time. They leave. Your flow sends a coupon. You trained them to wait for the discount — and you still did not explain the real total.

I work full-funnel ecommerce from Christchurch — SEO, Google Ads, Meta, CRO, Klaviyo. This is the abandon-cart pass I run before Q4 retainers get serious.

What the numbers usually say

Open Klaviyo (or your ESP) and Shopify checkout analytics side by side. Look for three tells:

  • Checkout starts are healthy, but payment step drops when shipping rates appear.
  • Abandon-cart email 1 recovers a few low-AOV carts; email 2 only moves when a code is attached.
  • Recovered orders still sit below your non-discounted AOV — you bought the sale back.

That pattern is not “email needs more creative.” It is mid-funnel honesty failing, with lifecycle papering over it.

💡 Pro Tip

Before you rewrite subject lines, pull 20 abandoned checkouts from last month. Note whether freight/GST was visible on the PDP or collection. If it was not, your flow is arguing with a surprise — not a preference.

Fix the storefront before you touch the flow

1. Put delivery truth above the fold on money pages. Rural NZ, AU interstate, Click & Collect, free-shipping threshold — say it on the PDP and the main collection, not only in checkout. A customer who already knows the freight is less likely to abandon at the rate screen.

2. Stop hiding GST as a gotcha. If your prices exclude tax until payment, say that early. If they include GST, say that too. Surprise tax is a trust leak; surprise tax plus a 10% code is a margin leak.

3. Kill one optional checkout field. Company name, second phone, “how did you hear about us” — each one is friction you do not need before Q4 volume. I have written about checkout UX before; this is the same rule, applied to abandon recovery.

Rewrite the abandon flow without a blanket discount

Email 1 (30–60 minutes): Plain reminder. Product, size/variant, and the shipping line they would have seen. No code. If freights starts from $X or free over $Y, put that in the body. Social proof only if it is specific (review on that SKU), not a generic star row.

Email 2 (next day): Still no automatic 10%. Offer help: Click & Collect, rural options, payment methods (Afterpay/Zip where you actually support them). Urgency only if inventory is real.

Discount, if any: Gate it. High-intent carts (viewed shipping, payment method started) get service copy. Low-intent window shoppers can get a time-boxed offer — not the whole list. Defaulting every abandon to a code trains your best customers to wait.

💡 Pro Tip

Run email 1 without a discount for two weeks in September. Compare recovery rate and contribution margin vs the coded version. Most stores overestimate how much of the recovery “needs” the code.

Wire paid and email so Q4 does not double-pay

If Meta or Google is sending cold traffic to PDPs that hide freight, you are buying abandons on purpose. Match landing pages to the promise in the ad. Exclude recent purchasers and active abandoners from prospecting where the pixel allows. Let Klaviyo own the cart; do not blast the same people a Meta retargeting carousel and a 15% email in the same hour.

Tracking check before you scale: Shopify, GA4, Ads, and Klaviyo should agree on a purchase. If they do not, stop optimising flows on a lie.

A September checklist (do this before Black Friday creative)

  1. PDP and top collections show freight threshold / GST stance in plain language.
  2. Abandon email 1 states shipping truth; no code by default.
  3. Discount only on a segment or email 2+, with an expiry.
  4. One checkout field removed; payment icons match what you actually offer.
  5. Paid landing pages match the same delivery story as the flow.
  6. Baseline recovery rate and margin for two weeks, then decide if the code ever comes back.

None of this needs a new ESP. It needs honesty on the storefront and a flow that does not bribe every hesitant cart.
