Keep Your Black Friday Page After the Sale (and Use the Same URL Next Year)

November 23, 2026 • SEO•3 min read

Black Friday is Friday. Most of the SEO work for this year's sale is already done or it isn't. But there's one decision left that pays off next November: what happens to your Black Friday page once the sale ends.

Some stores build a fresh page every year, like /black-friday-2026, then delete it or let it 404 in December. Each November they start ranking from scratch.

Google's advice is simpler. Use one page, keep it, and update it.

What Google actually recommends

Google's Search Central team wrote this down in its Black Friday best practices, and repeated it in its deals page guidance the following year.

  • Use a recurring URL. Their example is /sale/black-friday, not /sale/2020/black-friday.
  • Create it well before the sale, so Google has time to find and index it.
  • Link to it from your homepage, so shoppers and Google can reach it easily.
  • Put the event in the page title and describe it in the copy. The year can go in the title, just not the URL.

The point of all this is that links, rankings and history build up on one address. A new URL every year throws that away.

If your page has the year in the URL

Four days out, I wouldn't move this year's live sale page. Changing URLs mid-campaign risks broken ad links and email links, and that costs more than it saves.

Do it in December instead. Set up a plain /black-friday page (or /pages/black-friday on Shopify), then 301 redirect the dated pages from past years to it. That way any links and rankings those old pages earned point at the one you'll keep.

On Shopify, URL redirects live under Content → Menus → View URL redirects. One catch: Shopify only redirects an address that no longer works, so delete or rename the old page first. If you rename a page's URL handle, Shopify offers to create the redirect for you.

After Cyber Monday, keep the page live

Don't delete it, and don't let it 404. As Search Engine Roundtable reported, Google's John Mueller has said he'd keep these pages indexable after the event and simply make them less prominent on the site.

Here's what I'd put on it from December to October:

  • A short line that the sale has finished, and roughly when the next one usually runs.
  • A newsletter sign-up so people who land there hear about next year first. That turns a dead page into a list builder.
  • Links to your bestsellers or current offers, so a visitor in March still has somewhere to go.

Take it out of your main navigation and homepage, but leave it in your site and sitemap.

Next October

Update the same page with the new offers well before the sale, put it back on the homepage, and check it in Search Console with the URL Inspection tool to make sure Google can crawl it.

Because it's the same URL, everything it earned this year carries straight over.

Point your Google Ads, Meta ads and emails at that same address too. Every campaign then sends people, and links, to one page instead of a different one each year.

What to do this week

  1. Check your Black Friday page is linked from your homepage and can be crawled. Run it through URL Inspection in Search Console.
  2. Make sure the page title names the event, so it's clear what the page is about.
  3. Write the "sale's over" version of the page now, so it's ready to swap in on 1 December.
  4. Add a calendar note for December to set up a permanent URL and redirect last year's pages to it, if you need to.
