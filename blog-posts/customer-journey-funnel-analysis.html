How to Master Your Website's Customer Journey: A Guide to Funnel Analysis

Published: January 2025•8 min read

Running an online store isn't just about driving traffic—it's about understanding what your visitors do once they land on your site. Do they browse and leave? Add to cart but abandon? Or move all the way through checkout and convert into paying customers? The key to answering these questions is analysing the customer journey using a funnel.

What Is a Funnel?

Think of a funnel as the path customers take on your website—from the very first click to the final purchase. Just like a physical funnel, the number of people decreases at each stage. By analysing the drop-off, you can spot where customers are losing interest or hitting friction.

A typical e-commerce funnel looks like this:

  • Website Visit – unique visitors who land on your site.
  • Product Views – people who view one or more product pages.
  • Add to Cart – shoppers who add items to their basket.
  • Checkout Started – customers who begin entering shipping or payment information.
  • Purchase Completed – paying customers at the bottom of the funnel.

Why Is a Funnel So Important?

Instead of guessing why sales are low, a funnel gives you data-driven answers. It moves you from "We need more traffic!" to "Our checkout process is too complicated on mobile." By breaking down the journey, you can see the specific stage where your marketing or user experience is failing.

How to Calculate Conversion and Drop-off at Each Step

  • Conversion Rate to the Next Step: percentage of users who moved from one stage to the next.
  • Drop-off Percentage: percentage of users who exited the funnel at that stage.

Example: 10,000 visits, 6,000 product views = 60% conversion (40% drop-off). 6,000 views, 2,000 add to cart = 33%. 2,000 carts, 1,200 checkout = 60%. 1,200 checkout, 900 purchases = 75%. Overall: 900 / 10,000 = 9%.

What the Numbers Tell You

Low Product View Rate

Visitors aren't finding what they want. Improve homepage navigation, simplify the main menu, and make product categories more visible.

High Add-to-Cart Drop-off

Customers are interested but something on the page changes their mind. Clarify pricing and shipping, improve images, and write persuasive descriptions.

Low Checkout Completion

This is one of the most expensive bottlenecks. Shorten checkout, offer guest checkout, add trust badges, and offer multiple payment options.

High Purchase Drop-off

Unexpected costs or a clunky payment form. Show shipping early, remove hidden fees, and simplify the payment form.

Essential Tools

  • Google Analytics (GA4): custom funnels under Explorations.
  • Shopify Analytics: built-in funnel view for sessions, carts, checkouts, and orders.
  • Heatmaps & session recordings (Hotjar, Lucky Orange, Microsoft Clarity).

Why This Matters

By regularly tracking and improving your funnel you can increase revenue without increasing ad spend, identify bottlenecks that frustrate customers, and test new strategies with a clear measurement plan. Every small conversion-rate increase compounds into more sales at the end of the funnel.

Pro tip: Segment by device, traffic source, and customer type. A poorly optimized mobile checkout or a high bounce rate on one ad campaign will show up immediately.
